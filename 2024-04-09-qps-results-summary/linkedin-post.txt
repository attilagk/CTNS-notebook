Easy drug prioritization by Bayesian data fusion across multiple assays.

Preclinical studies often aim to find the most promising drug candidates out of a broader panel of drug treatments.  To this end, the panel is typically investiageted in multiple assays.  This prompts a data fusion question: What is the best way of aggregating evidence across the assays in order to prioritize drug treatments?

Classical, frequentist, statistics struggle with such evidence aggregation. In particular, p-values on different assays cannot be combined under broad conditions.

In contrast, Bayesian model averaging affords a normative way of data fusion that is both easy and optimal.  To illustrate its application to drug prioritization, I present a case study from an earlier work with Tina Loeffler and Madhav Thambisetty.

In this study, we evaluated approx. 50 drug treatments in several cell based assays relevant to Alzheimer's disease.  For each drug treatment and assay, the evaluation involved quantifying the support for a neuroprotective hypothesis with a Bayes factor.  The figure shows Bayes factors (on a log-scale) across all drug treatments (y-axis) and a subset of three assays of various amyloid-beta isoforms.  We see that for any given treatment, the assays support the neuroprotective hypothesis to a varying degree, which complicates drug prioritization.

Bayesian model averaging turns this complexity into simplicity with a remarkable ease: we can simply aggregate log-Bayes factors by averaging them without introducing more statistical bias.  The full blue circles mark the aggregate Bayes factors.

Data fusion achieved: the drug treatments have been prioritized.
